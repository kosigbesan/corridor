01The problem

Two categories nobody owns

For most companies the largest Scope 3 number is purchased goods. But the two categories with the least ownership and the most available data are business travel and employee commuting.

Sustainability teams report them once a year from a spreadsheet. Travel managers own the spend but not the number. HR owns the commute but not the reporting. Nobody holds the thread between them.

Meanwhile the data already exists, in expense platforms, booking tools and HR systems the company runs today. It has simply never been assembled.

Only about 37% of large firms disclose business travel at all, and roughly the same share set a travel-specific target.
The category is under-served by attention, not over-served by tools.

02The insight

The categories a company both causes and can see

Most carbon data is estimated from spend. These two categories arrive as itineraries, addresses and office days. That makes them measurable, movable, and from 2027, mandatory.
A mandate, not a choice

California SB 253 phases Scope 3 in from 2027 with business travel and commuting among only five required categories, and limited assurance on top.

A lever that moves fast

Cabin policy, trip substitution and office-day patterns change the number within a quarter. No other Scope 3 category responds this quickly.

Data the company owns

The feeds sit in systems already paid for. Nothing new has to be procured to start measuring, only assembled.
